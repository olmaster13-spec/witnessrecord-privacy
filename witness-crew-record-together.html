Witness Crew: record together on several iPhones

One shared clock, every angle in one place, and no Witness server in between.

Crew lets several iPhones film the same event into one shared case. This page explains exactly what it does and what it does not do, in the order you would use it. It is free and is part of Witness 1.1.13 for iPhone. Crew is available on iPhone. It is not available on Android yet. Written by the developer of Witness Camera, so read it with that in mind; where another approach is better, it says so.

Short answer. A crew is a small group of Witness users who filmed one event. Members join by scanning a code or opening a link, the phones match their clocks, every recording is stamped with its place on the crew's clock, and each recording's folder is shared with the others. Afterwards you can watch every angle in sync, cut between them and export one video, or export multicam timelines for Final Cut Pro, Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve. Footage still goes to each member's own cloud; Witness has no server.

What a crew is

A crew is a name for one event and a list of the phones filming it. The organizer, whoever started it, has the clock the others are measured against. Everyone who joins adds a name and an email so the crew can see who is in and share footage with them. New recordings join the active crew, and you can leave a crew to stop that without losing the recordings already in it.

Joining

  • Code: the organizer shows a QR code. Others scan it with the iPhone Camera.
  • Link: the organizer sends an invite link, which opens in Witness. If Witness is not installed, the link page offers the App Store.
  • The link does not tell this website anything about the crew. The crew travels in the part of the link after the # sign, which browsers do not send to a server.
  • Anyone who has the code or link can join, so share it only with the people you trust. It contains the key that protects the crew's data.

How the clocks are matched

Phones on a crew screen near each other find each other over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth, with no internet needed, and compare clocks the way network time works: one phone sends its time, the other answers with its own, and the delay of the round trip is measured and allowed for. A short burst of exchanges is made and the best is kept. Measurements chain back to the organizer, so two members who never met still end up on one timeline. The Crew screen shows how close the match is, in milliseconds. Details are in why phone clocks disagree.

Until a phone has measured its clock against someone, it uses its own time, which the app describes as usually within a fraction of a second. Each recording's manifest records the crew it belonged to, the member and the offset that was applied.

Staying in step

  • Over the internet: the member list, who is filming now and the list of recordings travel through the app's public iCloud database, sealed with a key that only the crew has. Apple stores encrypted data and Witness has no server to see it. Any iPhone signed in to iCloud can take part, on the free tier too.
  • Nearby: phones with the Crew screen open also swap the same information directly, with no internet. This asks for Local Network permission, and only when the Crew screen is open. Nothing is broadcast during a recording.
  • Freshness: the app checks while the Crew screen is open, and about once a minute while you are recording into a crew, so a new member or recording can take up to a minute to appear.

Where the footage goes

Not through the crew. Each phone keeps uploading to its own storage as usual. For Google Drive and Dropbox, Witness then shares the folder of each crew recording with the other members' emails, in the same way it does for emergency contacts, and the Crew screen has a button to open it. Folders on Nextcloud or a WebDAV server cannot be shared automatically; the app says so next to those recordings. Members without an email on file do not receive footage.

Watching every angle

When a crew recording stops, each camera's video is made smaller, sealed with the crew's key and posted through the same iCloud database. Other members' phones fetch the previews they lack while the Crew screen is open. Previews cover up to the first 20 minutes of a recording. The full-quality footage stays in each member's own cloud. The previews are for watching and choosing angles, not for evidence.

The player puts every angle on one timeline: play, scrub, and tap a camera to cut to it at that moment. Playback follows your cuts, and where the camera you chose was not filming, it falls back to one that was. You can set In and Out points to work on part of a long event and zoom the timeline to them.

Exporting

  • One video: your cut, either the whole timeline or In to Out, rendered as a 16:9, 720p video with the sound of the angle chosen, then shared or saved to Photos. Built from the previews, so it is preview quality. Portrait and landscape clips are fitted whole inside the frame, with black where no one was filming.
  • Multicam timelines: a Final Cut Pro file with one angle per recording, and an older-format XML that Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve can import, with one video track per angle. A README lists each angle's start on the crew clock. The files contain no footage: they point at a folder layout of one folder per member, and your editor can relink by file name if your files are somewhere else.

I have built these timelines to the formats' published structure and placed angles to the nearest frame at 30 frames per second, but I have not opened them in every version of every editor. If an editor will not import one, tell me which and which version.

What Crew does not do

  • It is not available on Android yet, and every member needs Witness on iPhone.
  • It does not make footage admissible or prove anything by itself. A matched clock is a measurement with a stated margin, not a certificate. For independent time proof see trusted time-stamping.
  • It does not hide the recording indicator or the sound.
  • A crew started before 1.1.12 syncs only nearby until the organizer shows the code or sends the link again.
  • A phone that dies mid-recording stops counting as filming after a few hours.
  • There is no live view of other phones' cameras while recording. Watching happens after a recording stops.

Questions

Is Witness Crew free?

Yes. Crew is part of the free app. Pro adds capture quality and extras and is not needed for Crew.

Does Crew use a Witness server?

No. Witness has no server. The crew's list and previews are encrypted with a key held only by the crew and stored in Apple's iCloud, and each phone uploads footage to its own cloud.

Can I use Crew on Android?

Not yet. Crew is available on iPhone.

Can I edit crew footage in Final Cut Pro, Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve?

Crew exports a Final Cut Pro multicam file and an XML that Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve can import, with every angle placed on the crew clock. The files point at your downloaded footage, so you may need to relink by file name.

How accurate is the sync?

The Crew screen shows the measured match in milliseconds once phones have compared clocks. Phones that never met a crew member use their own time, usually within a fraction of a second.

Who can see my name and email?

Only the members of your crew. They travel inside the crew's code, link and encrypted sync.
