Record video on a weak connection: how Witness sends a preview first

Crowds, rural areas and congested networks, and what keeps footage from being lost.

Mobile networks fail exactly when many people need them: at large demonstrations, in crowds, in remote places. An uploading recorder has to behave sensibly when the connection is poor. This page explains what Witness does. Written by the developer of Witness Camera, so read it with that in mind; where another approach is better, it says so.

Short answer. When the connection is weak, Witness keeps each 15-second piece on the phone until your storage confirms receipt, retries failed uploads with exponential backoff and flushes the queue the moment connectivity returns. If the queue falls visibly behind, with several pieces outstanding or repeated failures, it encodes a small low-resolution copy of each new piece and sends that first, so something of every moment arrives during the event, then the full file replaces it. On a healthy connection no extra copy is made. High-quality settings such as 4K need a fast upload and can outrun a mobile network.

What happens as the network degrades

  1. Pieces queue on the phone. Nothing is deleted until your storage acknowledges it.
  2. Failures retry with exponential backoff, and retries are idempotent: they land on the same destination file, not a duplicate.
  3. A stand-in goes first. If several pieces are outstanding or uploads keep failing, Witness encodes a small low-resolution copy of each new piece and sends that, so something of every moment is safe early.
  4. The full file follows and replaces the stand-in once the network allows.

The numbers to compare against

A typical LTE connection gives 5 to 10 Mbps of upload, and 5G varies hugely with where you are standing. The app's own estimates: 1080p H.264 needs about 8 Mbps, 4K HEVC 10-bit about 51 Mbps, and ProRes far more. Below the rate your setting needs, the queue grows for as long as you keep recording.

What to do about it

  • For anything where getting footage out matters, use the free 1080p setting. It uploads fastest.
  • Test on the connection you will actually have.
  • Bring a power bank: recording and uploading on a congested network is the worst case for a battery.

What it cannot do

It cannot upload without any signal. With no connection at all, pieces wait on the phone, which is the situation the app exists to avoid. And the pieces still on the phone when it is taken are lost.

Questions

What happens to my video if there is no signal?

Pieces queue on the phone and upload as soon as connectivity returns. Nothing is deleted until your storage confirms receipt.

What is the low-resolution stand-in?

If the upload queue falls behind, Witness sends a small low-resolution copy of each new piece first, so something of every moment arrives early. The full file replaces it later.

Does it make extra copies on a good connection?

No. The stand-in is only made when the queue is visibly falling behind.

Why does 4K make this worse?

4K records faster than most mobile networks can send, so the queue grows while you film. The free 1080p setting uploads fastest.
