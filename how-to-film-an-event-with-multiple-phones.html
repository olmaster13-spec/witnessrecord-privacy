How to film an event with several phones

Positions, overlap, one clock and a shared folder, for a group that wants every angle.

One phone sees one side. What it misses, another one can catch: the start of an incident, a face, a badge number, the crowd behind. This is a plan for a small group that wants to cover an event together and still have usable footage afterwards. Written by the developer of Witness Camera, so read it with that in mind; where another approach is better, it says so.

Short answer. Agree roles and positions before you go, add a phone that stays wide, start everyone before the action, never leave a gap with nobody filming, and put every recording on a common clock and in a shared folder so each person's footage reaches the others as it uploads. On iPhone, Witness Crew handles the clock and the sharing.

Why film in a group

  • Coverage. A wide shot shows what led up to an incident. A close shot shows detail. A shot from behind shows what the others cannot see.
  • Corroboration. Several independent angles of one moment tend to be harder to dispute than one clip.
  • Redundancy. If one phone is dropped, taken or runs flat, the others still have the moment. In Witness Crew the folder of each recording is shared with the whole crew as it uploads, so if a phone is taken, the others already have its footage.

Before: roles and positions

  1. One wide phone that stays back and keeps the whole scene in frame. If you only get one clip that matters, it is usually this one.
  2. One or two closer phones for detail: what people are doing, what is said, what is held.
  3. One phone that watches the edges: where people arrive from, where they leave to, and any vehicles.
  4. Agree what nobody does. Nobody crosses a police line or blocks anyone for the shot. Filming does not make you immune from a dispersal order. See filming a dispersal order and how to film police.
  5. Agree what happens if someone is stopped. The others keep filming from a safe distance and the footage goes to the person you chose in advance.

Setting up a Witness crew (iPhone)

This is the order the app itself suggests. Crew is available on iPhone. It is not available on Android yet.

  1. One person opens the Crew screen from the people icon on the main screen, types an event name and starts a crew. Everyone adds their name and email so the crew can see who joined and share footage with them.
  2. The others scan the code with the iPhone Camera, or open the link the organizer sends. Anyone who has the code or link can join, so share it only with your group.
  3. Before you start, keep the Crew screen open near each other for a few seconds so the phones match clocks. Apart, the phones still work; they use their own time, which is usually within a fraction of a second.
  4. Connect cloud storage on the home screen first. Each phone uploads to its own Google Drive or Dropbox, and Witness shares each recording's folder with the other members' emails as it uploads. Folders on Nextcloud or a WebDAV server cannot be shared automatically.
  5. Record as usual. The phones that are filming show as filming now on the Crew screen of the others.

During: keep the cover continuous

  • Start before the action and keep going. A phone that stops and restarts creates a gap and a second file.
  • Stagger breaks if someone has to stop, so at least one phone is always running.
  • Keep the phone's sound on. It also helps a later audio sync as a backup to the shared clock. See how to sync videos from multiple phones.

After: watch, choose, hand over

  • Open the crew in Witness and watch all the angles together on the phone, or export a video of your cut. Witness Crew explains each option.
  • Keep every original in its owner's cloud untouched. The previews and the exported cut are for watching and sharing, not the primary record.
  • Write down who was where and when, while you remember.
  • Give the originals to someone who can use them: a lawyer, a legal-observer group or a news desk. See what to do with protest footage.

Limits and trade-offs

  • More phones means more people who can be identified, stopped or asked to unlock a phone. Weigh whether a smaller group is safer.
  • Everyone in a crew sees each other's name and email. Use a name you are comfortable sharing with the group.
  • Witness cannot hide the green or orange recording indicator on iPhone, and always records sound.
  • This is general guidance, not legal advice. Rules on filming differ by country; see recording laws.

Questions

How many phones should film an event?

As many as you can manage safely and no more. Two or three with different jobs, one wide and one or two close, covers most of the value. More phones add coverage and add people at risk.

How do I make sure the videos line up?

Put the phones on one clock before you start, or make a loud clap on every phone and sync by audio afterwards. Witness Crew on iPhone matches clocks and stamps every recording with its offset.

What if one of our phones is taken?

If it uploaded while recording, most of its footage is already in its owner's cloud. In Witness Crew the recording's folder is also shared with the other members' emails, on Google Drive and Dropbox, so they already have access.

Do all members need the same app?

For Crew, yes: each member needs Witness on iPhone. Videos from any other phone can still be added to an edit by syncing them by audio.
